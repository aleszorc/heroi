# HEROI / ГЕРОЇ — Game Design Document

Brand: **HEROI / ГЕРОЇ** (icon and boot splash generated by `scripts/dev/make_branding.gd` into `assets/branding/`).

The battles centre on the *posadka* (Ukrainian *посадка*): the windbreak treelines that cross the steppe and became the most fought-over terrain of the war.

Mobile real-time tactics in an isometric 3D view (no player pause — deployment and menus freeze the sim instead). You command a reinforced Ukrainian platoon in short, tense battles. It draws on *Close Combat* (soldier psychology, suppression, morale) and *Defilade* (small maps, readable low-poly look, mobile-first controls).

---

## 1. Vision and pillars

1. **You are always watched.** Drones turn concealment into the main resource. A unit that moves, fires or stands in the open gets spotted, then hit by FPVs or artillery. Trees, trenches and ruins mean survival.
2. **Soldiers are people, not hit points.** Fire suppresses before it kills. Squads get pinned, cower and break. Your orders compete with fear.
3. **Short battles, real decisions.** Each battle takes 8–10 minutes (hard cap at 10), so it fits a commute. A battle has a few big choices (where to defend, when to spend the mortar, what to hit with the one FPV left), not hundreds of clicks.
4. **Readable at a glance on a phone.** Team discs, clear state colours, chunky low-poly silhouettes, an always-visible roster.

### Tone and sensitivity
- The war is real and ongoing. The game is sober, not gleeful: no gore, no kill-cams, no score popups for kills. Bodies stay on the field.
- Places, units and people are **fictional**. Equipment is real and depicted accurately and generically. The game does not recreate named real battles or real brigades.
- The player commands Ukrainian forces. The opponent is presented as an army, not as caricatures. The game never uses "Z" or "V" symbolism on enemy assets.
- Monetisation stays clean (premium or premium plus campaign DLC). No loot boxes or pay-to-win. Consider donating a share of revenue to humanitarian demining or medical charities and say so in the store listing.

## 2. Core loop

```
Campaign map ─► Briefing & deployment ─► Battle (real-time) ─► Debrief
     ▲                                                                     │
     └──── replace losses, spend requisition, promote veterans ◄───────────┘
```

- **Battle loop (seconds):** spot, then decide (where to send units, what to attack, when to call support), then watch suppression and morale play out, then react. The units handle their own tactics.
- **Campaign loop (minutes):** an operation is a run of 6–9 battles along a front ribbon (§2b). Casualties, veterancy and support charges carry over. Winning or losing a battle shifts the front and sets the next battle's kind and start line.

## 2b. The campaign

*Design settled and implemented 2026-10-25 (`scripts/data/campaign.gd`, `scripts/ui/campaign_screen.gd`). Still to come from M3: the extra map biomes and the curated Operation "Steppe".*

The campaign is **one operation played as a run**: 6–9 battles along a strip of front, generated from a single campaign seed, with the player's platoon persisting between battles. A campaign takes about an hour of play, is resumable mid-run, and is replayable by seed — the same identity random battles already have.

### 2b.1 The front ribbon
- The operation's map is a **ribbon of sectors** (treeline belt, hamlet, gully crossing, …) drawn as a strip, not a strategic map. Each sector maps to a `MissionGen` kind, and later to a biome — the M3/M4 map types (village, industrial outskirts, river crossing) slot in as sector types.
- Each battle's outcome moves the line: win an assault and the next sector opens; lose and the next battle is a **defend on the ground just taken**. Failure bends the front; it does not end the run.
- Where the front allows, the player chooses between **two sectors** for the next battle (push the hamlet = assault, or meet the counterattack = meeting/defend) — the agency of a strategic map without its cost.
- The operation ends in victory when the final objective sector is taken, and in defeat only when the platoon is combat-ineffective.

### 2b.2 Persistence
- **The platoon is the save file.** Killed soldiers are gone permanently and listed on a memorial; the wounded sit out 1–2 battles and return; replacements arrive green.
- **Veterancy is per squad, not per man:** surviving battles raises morale cap and suppression recovery — never accuracy. Per soldier the game tracks battles survived, wounds and rescues made; there are no kill counts anywhere (tone, §1).

### 2b.3 Requisition
- One currency, earned from battle results and objectives held.
- It buys replacements (green), support charges (mortar missions, FPV drones, bomber sorties) and one-off assets (Bradley repair, an extra team).
- **Disbanding** releases a unit from the roster and refunds half its purchase price, rounded down (`Campaign.DISBAND_REFUND`); replacements bought into it refund nothing, and its survivors are not fallen, so they do not go to the memorial. The last unit cannot be disbanded — the platoon always keeps one deployable unit.
- **Support charges are campaign ammunition** — no longer refilled free each battle. "When to spend the mortar" becomes a campaign decision as well as a battle one.

### 2b.4 Between battles
- One screen, in the spirit of the deployment screen: casualty report and memorial, requisition spend, sector choice where available.
- Each roster row carries its buy (+MAN) and DISBAND buttons; destructive taps (disband, new operation) use a two-tap confirm on the button itself (it turns into "SURE? +nRP" for three seconds, matching NEW OPERATION's "SURE? TAP AGAIN") rather than a modal dialog. Buttons the player cannot afford are disabled, and recruit buttons carry a one-line strength/role hint.

### 2b.5 Operation "Steppe"
- The authored campaign (M3) is a **curated run** on this system — fixed seeds, written briefings, a scripted final battle — the same relationship the curated start-menu scenarios have to random battles.

### 2b.6 Technical shape
- Campaign state (seed, front ribbon, roster, requisition, support stock, memorial) is one JSON-safe dict held in the `Game` autoload and serialized to `user://campaign.json` (`Campaign.save_state`/`load_state`; JSON floats are coerced back through `Campaign.geti`).
- `Campaign.dress_mission` rewrites a generated mission's title, briefing, UA platoon and support stock from the state: each `ua_units` entry gains a 4th element (roster index, deployable men, veterancy) that `Battle.spawn` passes into `Unit.setup` and `Campaign.apply_result` reads back at battle end — the debrief writer that folds casualties, wounded timers, veterancy, requisition and the front shift into the state and saves it the moment the battle ends (committed from `Hud._on_ended`, so leaving the end screen cannot dodge a defeat; abandoning a battle mid-way commits nothing and it can be refought).
- Veterancy applies in `Unit.setup` (morale bonus) and `_update_morale` (suppression recovery multiplier) from `Defs.VETERANCY`; `Support` reads its stock from `mission["support"]`.
- The campaign screen is built in code like `menu.gd`, over the same unit-less backdrop board.
- All campaign balance (requisition income and prices, veterancy levels, wounded-out duration, operation length) lives in `Defs.CAMPAIGN` / `Defs.VETERANCY`.
- Test hook: `godot --headless --path . --fixed-fps 30 --quit-after 6000 -- autostart fast log campaign [seed=N]` plays the saved campaign's next battle (first option) and commits the debrief; repeated runs walk one operation battle by battle. `-- campaign screen shots=/dir` captures the campaign screen.

## 3. The battle

### 3.1 Map
- Grid model of 120×120 cells, 2 m each (240 m square), shown as a diorama board.
- Terrain types and their effects:

| Terrain | Cover | Concealment | Infantry cost | Vehicle cost | LOS kept / cell |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Open | 0 | .10 | 1.0 | 1.0 | 1.0 |
| Field | .05 | .20 | 1.2 | 1.3 | 1.0 |
| Road | 0 | 0 | 0.9 | 0.6 | 1.0 |
| Trees (posadka) | .55 | .75 | 1.5 | 4.0 | .5 (every cell counts) |
| Building | .70 | .80 | 1.6 | impassable | blocks |
| Trench | .75 | .65 | 1.1 | 3.0 | 1.0 |
| Gully | .45 | .50 | 1.3 | 2.5 | 1.0 |
| Crater | .35 | .30 | 1.4 | 2.0 | 1.0 |
| Rubble | .50 | .50 | 1.6 | 3.0 | .85 |
| Wreck | .60 | .50 | 1.3 | impassable | .5 |

- Line of sight ignores the cells within 2 of either end (seeing out of and into windows, past the wreck a man shelters behind) — except trees: every tree cell on the line counts, so a wood 3 or more cells deep cannot be seen or shot through, a man two cells inside it is seen at a quarter of the open-ground chance and one three cells deep not at all. The observer's own edge of the wood is open to him: standing in trees, the tree cells next to him are free, so a man just inside the edge sees and shoots out at full visibility while fire coming the other way must get through his trees.
- Trenches are dug fieldworks, not dips: a sheer-sided cut with a flat floor at standing depth — about 1.7 m of real digging, drawn 2.1 world units deep to match the soldiers' 1.25× scale — so a standing soldier is roughly head-level with the ground. The walls are near-vertical dark revetment faces standing on the cell boundary (drawn by the view, with the lip kept crisp), and the spoil berm rises on every lip. Men crossing the lip play a quick hop down into the cut or a slower clamber out over the revetment (briefly slowed and unable to fire); movement along the floor is ordinary walking. Gullies stay smooth banks, sunk 1.4.
- Trenches are dressed with sandbag fieldworks derived from the cut's own geometry (`MapView._build_fieldworks`): a two-course parapet runs along the berm crest of every enemy-facing lip (92% of cells; north for defend/meeting, south for the enemy's trenches in assault), with a three-course firing bay about every third metre; a thinner single-course parados sits behind (45%); a bag wall caps each open trench end; now and then a stack revets the inner face of the fire-side wall at floor level, and loose bags lie dropped on the spoil. Four bag meshes (parapet run, firing bay, loose bag, low run), uneven in size, yaw and colour, drawn as four MultiMeshes with per-instance tone.
- Craters are premade rimmed-dish meshes in the dark wet-earth family of the trench spoil: 8 variants (rim radius 1.1–2.4 m, saucers with low lips through deep bowls under heavy spoil rims, varying spoke counts and elliptical asymmetry), each instance with random yaw, non-uniform XZ scale and a per-instance tone. They are scattered on painted CRATER cells at map build (in per-variant MultiMeshes) and spawned per big blast at runtime, scaled with the blast radius (FPV small, artillery big).
- Crater meshes never stack: a registry of live craters merges a new hole whose centre lands closer than 0.55× the summed rim radii to an existing one — that crater grows ~16% per hit toward a 4 m rim-radius cap, darkens toward a wet-mud floor tone and drifts slightly toward the new impact, so repeated shelling of one spot deepens one churned pit. Distinct-but-touching craters still coexist, like a real crater field.
- Under each mesh the heightfield keeps only a shallow scorched dimple (a lone hit ~0.3 deep, repeated hits saturating at 0.6, rim lift capped at 0.12), vertex-coloured from scorched char through disturbed earth: the mesh sits in a slight hollow, soldiers duck into it and the ground never z-fights the mesh. Painted CRATER cells get the same dimple at build. Only soft ground (open, field, road, crater) deforms; buildings, trenches, gullies and treelines keep their heights.
- Big explosions create new craters at runtime, so cover changes as the battle goes on: the ground mesh is chunked (10×10 chunks of 12×12 cells) and a blast rebuilds only the chunks it touches.
- Smoke grenades cut visibility to 30% per cell crossed for about 35 s.

### 3.3 Commands and autonomy (touch)
Two orders only: **tap ground = move there**, **tap a spotted enemy = attack it**. There is no order menu and no order modes; every other tactical decision is the unit's own, re-checked against the situation twice a second:

| Decision | What the unit does on its own |
|---|---|
| **Pace** | Runs (4 m/s, no firing, easy to spot) when no known enemy is within 200 m; walks and bounds by fire teams (2.2 m/s, fires at half accuracy) in contact; creeps (1 m/s, +10% concealment, fires only under 40 m) when closing on a known enemy within 80 m while still unseen |
| **Guard** | On arriving anywhere it settles in facing the threat (nearest known enemy, else the enemy map edge): men take cover against that direction and prefer targets inside the ±45° arc; vehicles pivot their front armour to it |
| **Hold fire** | If it settles concealed and unseen it waits in ambush (+15% concealment) until an enemy is within 60 m (150 m for vehicles) or it is fired on, then springs |
| **Smoke** | A squad pinned in the open pops a smoke screen towards the shooter (limited, 30 s between throws) |
| **Reposition** | Local only: in poor cover (< .5) it scrambles to cover away from an FPV homing in on it — dug in, it stays down instead — and shifts a few metres to better cover under sustained fire; it never abandons the area it was sent to |

Vehicles reverse automatically on short moves to the rear, keeping their front armour towards the enemy. TOW and Stugna fire only when stationary. An explicit attack tap overrides target choice and breaks hold-fire. If the target is out of reach (beyond 85% of the weapon range that counts against it — the anti-armour weapons against a vehicle — or without line of sight), the unit walks to the nearest spot it can fire from, trading a metre of walking for a tenth of cover, then settles facing the target and opens fire; it re-picks the spot if the target moves 20 m away (`Defs.ATTACK`). A pinned unit keeps the order and sets off once it can move; panic, a move tap or a new order cancels it.

### 3.4 Spotting
- Each unit is re-checked several times a second against every enemy observer within 260 m.
- Chance per 0.5 s = visibility × (1 − concealment) × range falloff, doubled if the target was seen in the last 6 s.
- Concealment rises when stationary (+.12), when the men lie flat (+.20, kneeling +.05) and falls when running (×0.5) or firing (×0.35 for 3 s, scaled by the weapon's *signature*: a rifle burst 1, a single sniper shot 0.3, so the sniper team keeps ~80% of its concealment after firing). The sniper team adds +.10 stealth. Vehicles are ×0.5, and ×0.4 again while moving. (`Defs.STEALTH`, weapon `signature`, unit `stealth`.)
- Anything within 10–45 m (depending on concealment) with line of sight is spotted automatically.
- Lost contacts show a **?** at their last known position for 45 s.
- **Shoot-and-move** (`Defs.DISPLACE`): automatic for all infantry — firing reveals the position, so after 3 shots from one spot a squad sneaks 15–25 m to the best concealed cell away from the known threat, then settles back into its old facing and ambush. It holds instead of moving when dug in (a trench or a building is never abandoned for a bush), and once the enemy has it spotted it only moves if the new spot actually breaks line of sight. It never trades down — a spot with worse concealment than the current one is refused, so a team shifts along its treeline rather than crawling into a field. An explicit player move order cancels it; pinned or panicked units never displace.
- Recon drones reveal everything in a 55 m radius with high probability, except units in buildings.

### 3.5 Shooting
`hit = acc × state × range_falloff × (0.4 + 0.6·visibility) × (1 − cover)^1.8 × posture`, with ×1.15 if the target is running.
Cover is the better of the ground cover and the directional shield (wall, wreck). Ground cover faces the threat the unit took it against (`Defs.COVER`): it counts in full inside ±60° of that facing and falls to 40% of its value from straight behind, so fire from the flank or rear of a trench or a wood edge bites far harder; buildings protect all round. With the power curve a trench (.75) keeps 8% of the hit chance, trees (.55) 24%, a crater (.35) 46% — a dug-in team is nearly immune to small arms from its front.
Target choice scores by distance, +80 m outside the guard arc, and +40 m per man a team is short of three (`Defs.TARGETING`): a two-man team far off is a poor pick next to a closer squad.
Misses scatter and still suppress whoever is nearby; a missed rocket or shell lands 2.5× further off than a missed bullet, usually outside its own blast radius. Blasts respect cover too: a rocket or tank round fired across the ground is stopped by the same (1 − cover)^1.8 curve as bullets, while a shell or drone coming from above still reaches into a trench at ×(1 − .65·cover) — and a dug-in man who is down (prone or ducked, in ground cover ≥ .6: a trench or a building, whichever side the shell lands) keeps only 35% of that again (`Defs.COVER` dugin_keep): the shrapnel sweeps over the lip while the suppression still lands in full. Infantry hits are 55% killed, 45% wounded (both out of the fight).
Armour: penetration × (0.85–1.15) against front, side or top armour, chosen by impact angle (front within ±50°). A penetration rolls for destroyed (55%), immobilized, weapons out, or crew casualty.

### 3.6 Suppression and morale
- Every shot near a unit adds suppression (`Defs.SUPPRESSION`). The volley's suppression is scaled by how well aimed it is (×0.5–1 for the shooter's state and ×0.5–1 for range), then by cover twice: ×(1 − .85·cover) at the impact (directional, as for hits) and ×(1 − .5·cover) at the unit (all incoming fire, blasts included). A trench keeps about a quarter of rifle-fire suppression; a man in the open takes all of it.
- It decays 7–21 per second after 1.5 s without incoming fire, faster in cover — and a unit in cover keeps recovering even while fire is still coming in, at cover × 0.6 of that rate (men duck below the lip between bursts), so a dug-in team cannot be pinned by a squad shooting at it from 150 m; it takes close or heavy fire, or HE.
- States: **Ready** → **Suppressed** (>28: 60% accuracy, 75% speed) → **Pinned** (>60: won't move, 30% accuracy) → **Panicked** (>90, for 7–12 s). A panicked unit never runs across a field (`Defs.PANIC`): with any real cover against the threat (≥ .3 directional) it *cowers* where it is; in the open it dashes at most 8 m to the best cover close by and cowers there. When the panic passes it crawls back to the post it was holding and takes up its old facing (a unit that broke while on the move settles where it stopped; the move order is lost).
- Morale starts at 55–80 and drops with casualties (−10), nearby friendly losses (−2 per man, −6 per unit) and panic. Thresholds scale with morale. At 12 or below (`Defs.PANIC` rout_morale) a **Russian** unit routs: it stops fighting and makes for its own map edge, and counts as gone for its wave. Ukrainian units never rout or leave the map: with shattered morale they break into panic sooner, but stay and recover. (A/B with the bot over 40 battles: enemy-only rout won 31, rout for both sides 30, no rout 20.)

### 3.6b Soldier bodies, stances and cover
- **Rig and look:** every soldier is a skinned 19-bone low-poly rig, posed procedurally each frame. The figure follows the clean low-poly reference style: few large volumes with strong color blocking (shirt / trousers / charcoal gear / skin) rather than greebles. Gear is limited to what reads at game zoom: plate carrier with one pouch row, chest radio with antenna, thigh holster and pouch, integrated dark knee pads, headset helmet with NVG mount and side rails. Ukrainians wear rolled sleeves (bare forearms) and a yellow armband; Russians full sleeves and red. Joint-filling balls match their surrounding color exactly, so they are invisible until a limb bends and they close the gap.
- **Gait (foot-planted IK):** each foot is a world-space point nailed to the ground through its stance half of the stride and stepped to a predicted landing in its swing half; two-bone IK bends the knee to reach it. Feet cannot slide and knees bend exactly as much as the step demands — walking, running and crouch-walking all come from the same system, with the body sinking as each foot passes under it and weight shifting over the stance foot. Standing men whose feet were left behind or twisted by a turn take one small step home, one foot at a time, so turning in place reads as a shuffle. Prone, crawling and kneeling remain scripted poses.
- **Stances:** kneeling, prone, ducked behind cover, rising to fire, cowering, and falling when hit.
- **Hit physics:** a killed man is knocked along the incoming shot's direction and falls with his own randomized rest pose, fall speed and yaw twist, so no two bodies settle alike. Blasts throw bodies in a ballistic arc with a limp tumble that blends out as they land. Bodies stay where they fall.
- **Movement physics:**
  - A squad's leader point follows the path and slows when men lag more than 4.5 m behind.
  - Each man steers to his formation slot with limited acceleration and eases into a stop.
  - Men keep 1.1 m apart (more when prone) and slide along walls. No man ever crosses an intact-wall cell — not steering to a slot, diving for cover, being dragged, or thrown by a blast: every per-soldier step goes through one wall gate, so buildings are entered only through doors, breaches or window climbs (window climbs cap a man at 40% speed). Cover search never offers a spot on the far side of an intact wall.
- **Stance rules** (each man decides from his own position, relative to the nearest known threat):
  - **Trench, building or rubble, or behind a wall or wreck:** he ducks and only rises when he has a target. A ducked man must be up before he can fire.
  - **Open ground**, or when suppressed or guarding: he goes prone.
  - **Trees or gully:** he kneels.
  - **Moving while suppressed:** he crawls.
  - **Panicked:** he cowers in place.
- **Directional cover:** obstacles 1–3 m towards the shooter add protection (wall .85, wreck .7, rubble .55, trees .3). Cover selection maximises that against the threat and is re-checked when the threat moves. The ground cover itself (trench, trees, gully…) is also directional: it was taken against the threat and gives only 40% of its value against fire from behind (§3.5).
- **Posture modifies hit chance:** crouched ×0.7, prone ×0.5.

### 3.6b-2 Soldier detail and squad movement
- **Figure:**
  - About 1,000 triangles on a 19-bone rig: hips, spine, chest, head, upper and lower arms, hands, thighs, shins, feet, and a weapon bone.
  - Faceted tapered limbs, knee pads, cargo pockets, boots with soles, battle belt.
  - Plate carrier with magazine pouches and radio, helmet with goggles and NVG mount, side tape (Ukrainian yellow and blue, Russian red).
  - Four gear variants, which change shades, pack and balaclava.
- **Grounding:** every soldier casts a soft radial contact shadow on the terrain (wider when prone or fallen), and running heel strikes kick puffs of dust — the figures sit on the ground rather than hover over it.
- **Weapons**, modelled from their real layout:
  - AK-74: curved magazine (convex edge towards the muzzle), plum for Russian troops; black furniture with rail and red dot for Ukrainian.
  - PKM: ammo box and folded bipod.
  - SVD: thumbhole stock and PSO-1 scope.
  - RPG-7: slim tube with wooden heat guard and flared venturi; PG-7 grenade with needle fuze, 85 mm ogive bulb and boat-tail booster.
  - The Stugna tube is carried on the back.
- **Weapon handling (researched carry positions):** three context layers — *relaxed* (no known enemy): each man's personal carry, one of muzzle-down patrol sling, Sul-style close carry or relaxed low carry, with the RPG humped on the shoulder; *alert* (enemy known within 220 m, suppressed, or fired in the last 12 s): low ready, stock in the shoulder, muzzle depressed; *engaged*: shouldered aim. Running blends to port arms, prone to prone aim. Every man gets personal ready angles, a slow weapon sway, a stride bounce, and a periodic grip-shift where the support hand drops to the magazine well — and patrol-sling carriers take the support hand off entirely, the free arm swinging with the stride. Two-bone IK keeps hands on grip and handguard in every pose; tracers leave from the real muzzle.
- **Body motion:**
  - Hips swing and the chest counter-turns; the body bobs twice per stride.
  - He leans into turns and acceleration, his head scans when not aiming, and he breathes and shifts weight at rest.
  - Going prone drops to the knees first.
- **Squad movement:**
  - Grid paths are string-pulled into straight runs.
  - Men follow the leader's breadcrumb trail and fall in instead of walking backwards. The squad picks a **situational formation** from context and re-checks it twice a second while moving: **wedge** in the open, **staggered column** when running, sneaking, or in trees, trenches and buildings, **line** when assaulting a known enemy ahead (within 90 m, roughly on the axis of advance), **dispersed** (double spacing) when suppressed or under shelling, and a tight **stack** file on the last 14 m into a building entrance.
  - **Leader hand signals:** the lead man plays short procedural signals, blended onto the right arm so they read at game zoom — he points the direction on a move order and on each bound, raises a fist (halt) on reaching the destination, and circles an arm overhead (rally) when the squad recovers from being pinned or panicked.
  - Each man has his own reaction delay (0.1–0.9 s) and pace (±7%).
  - Men in formation are fed the squad's velocity and only gently correct towards their place, so the file flows instead of surging and stopping. Spacing pushes are smoothed, and the leader slows gradually as men lag.
  - No team discs under units: sides read from arm tape, silhouettes and labels.
  - In contact on a normal Move, two fire teams bound 12 m in turn while the other covers from cover at full accuracy.

### 3.6b-3 Individual soldier behaviour (Defilade-style)
- **State machine:** every soldier runs an explicit behaviour state machine (`soldier.gd`, enum `ST`): IDLE, MOVING, FIRING, RELOADING, THROWING, DIVING (grenade), HIT_REACT (diving from effective fire), COWERING, RESCUING, HIT_FALLING, DEAD, WOUNDED, BEING_DRAGGED, STABILIZED and GETTING_UP, with a `walking_wounded` modifier on the upright states. The transition table is documented in the class header; the squad reads the state instead of ad-hoc flags.
- **Wounded:**
  - Half of bullet hits and a third of blast hits wound instead of kill.
  - The wounded lie on their backs, writhing, and bleed out after 120 s unless treated.
  - A man hit while lying prone stays as he lay: face down, same heading, no knock-back throw or spin, whether killed or wounded. Wounded face down, he draws a leg up to the side and claws slowly at the ground; he is dragged head first like any casualty and gets up with a straight push-up instead of first rolling onto his front.
  - A wounded man down on the ground can be finished off by a later blast.
- **Rescue and walking wounded:**
  - Each squad sends one free man (own casualties first, then any friendly within 65 m).
  - He crouch-runs to the casualty, slings his rifle, grabs the vest and drags the man backwards into the nearest cover away from the threat.
  - He then kneels and gives first aid for 8 s, after which the casualty is stabilized.
  - A stabilized man rests 10 s, then gets back up as **walking wounded**: he rejoins the fight limping, at 55% accuracy and 60% speed (`Defs.WOUNDED_FIGHT`), throws no grenades and is never sent on rescues. Going down again does not double-count his loss.
  - A new move order cancels the job.
  - The debrief reports killed, wounded and treated; the treated are the ones back on their feet.
- **Reacting to fire:** when effective fire lands on a squad (a suppression jolt ≥ 9 after cover), every free man dives within 0.1–0.3 s (his own delay) to the nearest micro-cover inside ~2.5 m — or simply flat, facing the threat — and stays low for ~4 s; men already flat or standing in trenches, buildings or behind walls just duck. The squad then adapts through the normal suppression logic: return fire, crawl, or bound (`Defs.REACT`).
- **Shell dive** (`Defs.SHELL_REACT`): every shell landing makes free men within three blast radii dive flat (90% per man, after his own split-second delay) and stay low for 5 s past the round. Each round of a fire mission re-extends the window, so a barrage catches a squad flat in its holes rather than upright.
- **Cover-to-cover rushes:** in contact on a normal Move, each man of the moving fire team picks his own cover spot a bound (12 m) ahead and sprints there crouched. The covering team holds the cover it last rushed to. The next bound starts only when every rusher is down.
- **Tactical walk:** with the enemy known within 220 m, walking men drop to a crouched walk.
- **Grenades:**
  - Riflemen carry two and lob one at enemy infantry 8–30 m away (35% chance per firing decision, one per squad every 8 s), with a wind-up and throw animation.
  - The grenade arcs, rolls and goes off after 3.6 s (6 m radius; also chips walls).
  - Men within 7 m who see it land (75%) dive flat away from it.
- **Reloads:** magazines run dry (AK 10 bursts, PKM 10, SVD 10 shots, RPG 1). The reload animation tilts the weapon, sends the support hand to the vest pouch and back, and blocks firing for 2.6–6 s.

### 3.6c Destructible buildings
- **Construction:** houses are 1 m wall segments, window sills and lintels, a door frame (posts and head beam) and a roof, on a plinth slab that outlives everything above it. Three roof styles: a **gable** roof (two slopes of three butted slabs whose undersides pass exactly through the wall-top edges and meet under a ridge cap, prism gable triangles flush under the slopes, a 0.3 m eave overhang), a **hip** roof (one lofted solid, every slope at the same pitch, short ridge cap), and for brick houses a **flat** roof behind a parapet. Intact gable and hip roofs usually carry a chimney. Each house has one door: a full grid cell of wall left out, so the visible gap and the walkable cell match. Windows sit every second or third metre with a per-house phase, never at corners (sill below, lintel above, open in between). Per-house variation: wall colour (whitewash, cream, pale green, ochre or blue-grey plaster; four brick tones), roof colour (tile, slate, rusty tin), window rhythm, chimney. Every piece has hit points: brick 130, plaster 90.
- **Ruined variants** from mission data (`ruin`): below 0.35 the roof is intact and some wall slots are built low; from 0.35 the roof has caved in — one or two slope sections lean from the top of a standing eave wall (built full height) down to the floor inside the footprint, the rest is gone and about half the walls are sooted; from 0.6 the roof is absent altogether. Floors carry rubble in proportion.
- **Support rules:** every piece is physically carried and drops as debris when its carrier goes (`_drop_unsupported`, cascading): a window lintel needs its sill and at least one full-height jamb beside it; the door head and posts need the jambs; a gable triangle or parapet needs at least half of the slots of the wall it stands on at full height; a caved slab needs the wall slots under its upper edge; the chimney needs its slab, the chimney cap the chimney; a ridge cap needs two slabs. A carrier chipped more than 0.25 m below its built top carries nothing. Pieces that would start unsupported (a gable over a mostly ruined end wall) are pruned at build.
- **Structural damage (power and radius):**

| Weapon | Power | Radius |
|---|---|---|
| 125 mm HE | 180 | 3 m |
| Artillery | 160 | ~4.7 m |
| ATGM | 90 | 2 m |
| RPG | 70 | 2 m |
| Mortar | 60 | 3 m |
| FPV | 35 | 1.5 m |
| Autocannon | ~20 | 1.2 m |

- **Hits on walls:**
  - Partial hits knock the top off a segment.
  - Lethal hits blow the piece out as 2–4 physics debris chunks. Rigid bodies settle and freeze after 8 s; a maximum of 160 active and 500 total.
  - Shells aimed at men inside a building hit the wall on the shooter's side 70% of the time.
- **Effect on the grid (entry rules):**
  - Infantry enter only through the door, a window, or a breach; intact wall cells block movement. Vehicles never enter.
  - Door and breach cells are freely passable. Window cells, and walls chipped to 0.8–1.2 m, are climbable by infantry only: 3× path cost and 0.4× speed while crossing.
  - Breached cells become rubble, which can be seen and walked through.
- **Structural failure:**
  - Below 60% standing walls, the roof caves in: every soldier inside the footprint is struck with 45% chance (no cover applies — the cover itself is falling), with the usual blast split of roughly a third wounded rather than killed; wounded men already on the floor can be crushed outright, and every unit in or near the house takes heavy suppression (50).
  - Below 30%, the house collapses into a rubble heap — worse again: 70% strike chance per man inside and suppression 70, on top of the cave-in if both thresholds fall in one blow.

### 3.6d Smoke and atmosphere
- **Puffs:** smoke, dust and flame are faceted 3D puffs (same style as the tree crowns), lit and shadow-casting. They are simulated with velocity, drag, buoyancy and wind, swell, then dither-dissolve. About 3,100 puffs in two MultiMeshes, written in one buffer per frame.
- **Smoke grenade:** the cloud grows from the canister outward — puffs spawn near-stationary inside a disc that widens to the sight-blocking radius over ~3.5 s and reach full size within ~2 s, so the frontier advances as one thick mass rather than as separate globes; a narrow billowing column keeps feeding it from the canister for about 35 s. The line-of-sight block drifts with the visible cloud.
- **Other effects:** explosions (flame, black smoke, dust ring, thrown clods), burning wrecks, missile trails, track dust and exhaust, and the muzzle dust ring of a tank gun.

### 3.6e Vehicle models
- **Construction:** extruded side-profile hulls and lofted turrets, built from published dimensions and running-gear layouts.
- **T-72B3:** 6 large road wheels, 3 return rollers, rear sprocket, Kontakt-5 on glacis and turret, sleeved 125 mm gun, Sosna-U sight, fuel drums, unditching log, anti-drone cage.
- **BMP-2:** wedge nose, ribbed glacis with folded trim vane, front sprocket, 2A42 cannon, Konkurs launcher on the roof, bulged rear doors.
- **MT-LB:** flat cab with armoured windows, small MG turret front right, six wheels with no return rollers.
- **M2A2 Bradley:** sloped glacis, applique tiles, spaced skirts, turret offset right with the TOW box on the left, bustle rack, rear ramp.

### 3.6f Vehicle physics
- **Driving:** acceleration and braking, slowing for turns and for the final stop.
- **Hull motion:** the hull follows the ground slope and rocks on a damped spring when accelerating, braking, turning, or under main-gun recoil (relative to turret direction).
- **Destruction:** a destroyed T-72 always throws its turret (ammunition cook-off); other vehicles do 15% of the time.

### 3.7 Drones
- **Recon (player):** tap an area. Orbits for 25 s, 45 s cooldown.
- **FPV drone model:** racing X-frame kamikaze quad — carbon plate, four arms with two-blade props, strapped lipo on top, tilted nose camera, whip antennas, RPG warhead slung underneath. The recon drone keeps a commercial quadcopter look.
- **FPV (player):** 5 per battle, 12 s cooldown. Tap a spotted enemy. 80% hit (55% vs trench or building, 70% vs moving vehicle, 65% vs moving infantry). Top attack with 350 penetration, plus 4 m blast.
- **Bomber drone model:** heavy agricultural-style hexacopter — olive tub on a carbon frame, six arms with big props, landing skids, up to four bombs on a belly rack that visibly empties.
- **Bomber (player):** 3 sorties, 20 s cooldown, needs the Drone Team. Tap a spotted enemy: the hexacopter crosses in from the Ukrainian edge (13 m/s at 22 m altitude), slows over the target and drops 3–4 bombs a second apart, each aimed at where the target is at release with ~3 m scatter. The ~2 s fall means vehicles and pinned or stationary infantry take the string full on while units that move largely escape. Each bomb: top attack with 45 penetration (kills IFVs outright, roughly even odds on a tank roof), 5 m blast, heavy structural damage — a string levels a house. After the last bomb it turns for home and despawns at the edge. Ground fire brings it down more easily than an FPV (big, slow, long overhead).
- **Enemy FPVs:** every 40–60 s against a recently spotted unit (priority: vehicles, then ATGM and MG teams, then targets in the open). The hunted unit scrambles for cover on its own; moving and cover reduce the hit chance. Infantry within 35 m can shoot it down. The rising whine is the warning cue — no marker.
- No drone team means no drones. Protecting the operators matters.

### 3.7b Scenarios (procedural)
- `MissionGen.generate(kind, seed)` builds a full mission from a seed: randomized steppe (treelines with gaps, fields, roads with poles, gullies, a hamlet of 3–8 buildings near an objective, craters, wrecks), three named victory locations, deploy zone, enemy plan, Russian fire plan and season. The same seed reproduces the same map and plan (combat itself stays random); kind and seed are shown on the briefing panel.
- **Victory locations never touch:** neighbouring centres are at least 40 m apart (`MissionGen.VL_MIN_DIST`, rings are 16 m), and all sit at least 25 m inside the map edge.
- **Season** (`mission["season"]`: summer, autumn, winter or mud, weighted 3:3:1:1, drawn from its own stream so maps found under a seed stay the same): the briefing carries a one-line weather note; the map view renders the palette. In mud, mechanized waves keep to the road where one leads towards their objective.
- **Enemy plan:** each kind rolls a named pattern per seed and builds its waves from it, so no two seeds play the same way. Waves vary by **entry edge** (north above the objective, a north corner, or partway down the east/west edge for a flank), **route style** (direct, hook round a flank, along the main road, along a gully), **composition** (mech: vehicle + 2–3 groups; probe: 2 groups, no vehicle; armour: 2 vehicles + 1 group; dismount: 3–4 groups with an MT-LB behind; tank: a lone T-72; feint: vehicle + 1 group that halts short) and **style** (the §7 behaviour hint). Patterns: defend — drumbeat (even beats, one heavy), lull_push (probe, long quiet, then armour and infantry from two directions), early_rush (an MT-LB rush just after the first minute with a flank probe behind it, regroup, then armour and a late flank), steady (mechanized pressure, last column round a corner); assault — counter, reinforce (fresh infantry joins the defence early), flank_counter (from the east/west edge), tank_overwatch; meeting — columns, race, echelon; town — house_to_house, street_rush, encircle (dismounts from both flanks). The pattern name is `mission["plan"]`.
- **Russian fire plan** (`mission["ru_support"]`, schema in `Support.DEFAULT_RU`): the prep barrage is none / short (5 shells) / full (10) / late (8 shells at 60–200 s), aimed at a mission-specific band — flags and trenches when we defend, the forming-up strip when we attack (never before 28 s, and the briefing warns), the contested middle or the approach when both race. FPV, artillery (4–8 shells) and recon first-strike times and cadences scale with a per-seed intensity of 0.7–1.3. The hand-built Treeline 7 carries no plan and gets the classic one (full barrage on the treeline band, FPV from 90 s every 40–60 s, artillery from 170 s every 120–160 s).
- **Every battle is capped at 10 minutes of sim time** (`Battle.TIME_CAP`); mission data may ask for less but never more. Wave schedules scale with each mission's `time_limit`, so pacing holds at any length.
- **Defend:** you hold three southern positions with trenches; 3–5 waves attack from the north on one of the plan patterns below (8–10 min). The platoon starts on cover: squads, MG and ATGM teams are snapped onto the trench line behind their flag (within 20 m of the intended spot), the sniper and drone team into the nearest treeline or grove (else a trench end or a house), so nobody starts prone in an open field facing the wrong way; the trenches lie south of the flags and the default facing is north. Which squad and which support team (MG, ATGM, sniper) holds which flag, and on which flank, is drawn from the seed; in assault and meeting battles the order along the deploy strip is shuffled the same way, so the platoon never starts in the same layout twice.
- **Assault:** the enemy is dug in on three northern positions from the start (hold-mode defenders, plus one counterattack wave); you deploy in the south and must take a majority before time runs out (9–10 min).
- **Meeting:** all three objectives start neutral in the middle band; enemy columns race for them from the first minute — whoever holds the majority at time-out wins (8–10 min).
- **Town:** a village on a street grid — main street, two or three cross streets and a lane, 12–20 houses along them (up to 80% ruined, rubble beside the worst), gardens instead of treelines, fields and a gully beyond — held like a defend mission (8–10 min). Objectives sit on crossroads and public buildings (Church, School, Market…); the enemy comes house to house from the north with more infantry and fewer vehicles. CLI `kind=town`.
- The start menu (`scripts/ui/menu.gd`, ГЕРОЇ wordmark over a unit-less generated backdrop) lists the curated scenarios from `scripts/data/scenarios.gd` — Treeline 7 plus eight vetted kind+seed presets (Tykhyi Brid, Stari Kopani, Vovche Pole, Zhuravlyne, Mlynove, Kachyne, and the town battles Lypove and Zelenyi Hai) — plus a CAMPAIGN entry (the §2b operation; CONTINUE CAMPAIGN when a save exists) and a RANDOM BATTLE entry (random kind and seed, both shown so a good map can be found again). Picking one lands on the map in deployment mode framed as the pre-battle screen (title, briefing, START BATTLE). The end screen offers PLAY AGAIN (same map), NEW BATTLE (new seed) and MENU; the in-battle menu button opens the options overlay (RESUME / RESTART / EXIT TO MENU / QUIT). CLI: `-- kind=assault seed=7` skips the menu.

### 3.8 Victory
- Victory locations (flags on poles; the cloth waves in the wind via a shared vertex shader, phase-offset per flag by world position, recoloured on capture through instance uniforms) are captured by being the only side with steady troops within 16 m for 8 s. Generated locations are placed at least 40 m apart, so capture rings never overlap and each flag is its own fight.
- **Win:** hold the majority of locations at time-out, or break every enemy wave.
- **Lose:** every unit destroyed, or the enemy holds the majority at time-out.

## 4. Controls and UI (landscape phone)

- **One finger:** tap a friendly unit to select it; with a unit selected, tap ground to move and tap a spotted enemy to attack — the only two orders. Drag to pan. There is no order menu, no order bar and no long-press. On desktop the mouse is handled directly, not through touch emulation. **Two fingers:** pinch to zoom, drag to pan. **Buttons:** rotate the view in 90° steps.
- **Selection:** a selected unit shows its name in yellow (no ground ring), one line only: its path as a ribbon while it moves (blue for a move, red while walking to a firing spot on an attack), otherwise its fire line to the target, and the fire arc it guards as a translucent cone (violet while holding fire, blue otherwise).
- **Top left:** clock and objectives (blue held, red lost, `!` contested).
- **Top right:** rotate, speed (cycles 1×, 2×, 4×), menu (☰ — opens the options overlay, which freezes the sim: RESUME restores the previous state, RESTART replays the same kind+seed, EXIT TO MENU returns to the start menu, QUIT closes the game). There is no player pause; `Game.paused` remains an internal mechanism for deployment, the options overlay and the end screen.
- **Bottom:** roster cards (name, men alive, state colour). Tap a card to select and centre the camera.
- **Right edge:** support (Mortar, Recon, FPV, Bomber) with charges and cooldowns; each arms a mode for the next tap, shown as a prompt with Cancel.
- The message feed carries outcomes only: battle start and end, objectives taken or lost, units destroyed or lost. No advice, warnings or contact reports — the world itself (FX, the FPV whine, roster state colours) is the feedback.
- **Desktop debug:** mouse acts as touch, wheel zooms, WASD pans, Q/E rotate, Esc backs out one layer (armed support mode → selection → options overlay).

## 5. Art direction

- **Diorama low-poly.** Every model is built in code from primitives, flat-shaded, vertex-coloured, one mesh per object. That means tiny builds, a single draw call per object, and no texture memory.
- **Palette by season** (`mission["season"]`, missing = summer; the start-menu backdrop takes its generated mission's season or a random one): **summer** — green steppe under ripe gold grain, green poplars and acacias, a high warm sun; **autumn** — the late-steppe olive grass, yellow-brown stubble fields, gold and rust crowns, low golden light; **winter** — snow over everything but the churned roads and trench spoil, bare twig-lattice trees and thickets, snow-dusted roofs, a pale low sun over a bright cool ambient; **mud** (rasputitsa) — dead dull grass, dark churned fields and muddy roads, bare trees, flat overcast light. Plaster is grey-white and ruins red-brick in every season. Saturation is held around 0.8–0.95 by season (`main.gd SEASONS`, `MapView.GROUND`, `MeshFactory.LEAF`).
- **Readability over realism:** soldiers drawn 25% oversize, blue or red team discs, yellow tape on Ukrainian soldiers and red on Russian (both real practice), white crosses on Ukrainian vehicles, cope cages on Russian tanks. Everything else stays real scale so the soldiers read against it: treeline poplars and acacias stand 7–14 m (real posadka height), vehicles, poles and houses are true size.
- **Effects:** tracers, rocket and missile smoke trails, flash plus smoke plus dirt explosions, persistent burning wrecks, craters.

## 7. AI

- **Waves:** each wave has a spawn time, a route, an objective and a **style** hint from the mission (`EnemyAI.STYLES`): assault (the baseline below), rush (closes to 90 m, fires 2–4 s between bounds, bounds 35 m and keeps going up to 55 suppression; vehicles push 14 s), probe (opens fire at 190 m, 8–14 s between short bounds, only under 20 suppression, and falls back a waypoint to cover once half the group is down or suppression passes 50), flank (between the two), overwatch and feint (the wave's route is cut at `stop_at`; it drives or walks there and fights from that point, never pushing into contact; feints also fall back when hurt). Waves with `hold` stay where they spawn.
- **Infantry (assault):** advance (run when no contact) → engage (halt and fire 5–10 s) → bound (rush ≤25 m to the best cover towards the next waypoint, only if suppression is under 35) → repeat → guard the objective. The AI gives the same two inputs as the player — destinations and halts — and the units' own autonomy does the rest.
- **Vehicles (assault):** advance, then overwatch whenever enemies are within 225 m for 8–14 s, then push for 8 s. They stop one waypoint short of the objective (right on it when the wave halts at a route point). A vehicle with its weapons knocked out retreats.
- **Support:** the fire plan comes from the mission (§3.7b); the fire director picks only from what the Russian side has actually spotted. The rule is the same as for the player: no cheating vision.
- **Indirect fire** (`Defs.ARTILLERY`): player mortar 6 rounds at 0.9 s, 9 m scatter, 8 m blast, 0.75 lethality, 60 suppression; Russian artillery 4–8 rounds at 1.3 s, 14 m scatter, 9 m blast, 0.70 lethality, 65 suppression, 220 wall damage (a 152 mm shell takes a brick wall down); prep barrage 8 m blast, 0.50 lethality, 50 suppression. Suppression is felt out to three radii, so a shell 10 m away pins a squad in the open; cover cuts the hit chance by up to 65% and suppression by 35%. Men dive flat on each round landing within three radii (§3.6b-3, `Defs.SHELL_REACT`), and a dug-in man who is down keeps only 35% of the top-attack lethality on top of the cover reduction while suppression lands in full (`Defs.COVER` dugin_keep) — so a fire mission on a trench squad usually pins it for 0–2 casualties, while the same rounds on a squad in the open are still crippling.

- Mounted infantry dismounting from BMPs.
- Reserve commitment when a wave stalls.
- Difficulty levels that change wave sizes and enemy morale, not accuracy.

- **Simulation** runs on its own clock, so pausing freezes impacts, artillery and drones while the camera and UI stay live. Large frames are split into 50 ms sub-steps.
- **Determinism:** every simulation roll (hit chances, scatter, AI and panic timers, drone strikes) draws from `Game.rng`, seeded per battle from the mission seed (`Game.seed_sim`, `-- sim=N` overrides it). Presentation randomness (fx, meshes, animation jitter, audio) stays on the global RNG, which `seed_sim` also seeds on a separate stream because the solved pose feeds `muzzle()`. Under the fixed-fps headless harness the same `kind`/`seed`/`sim` triple replays the identical battle log, so balance changes can be A/B tested by varying `sim=` alone.
- **Audio** is the one exception to everything-procedural: real CC0 recordings in `assets/audio/` (provenance in `assets/audio/SOURCES.md`), processed to mono WAV one-shots and MP3 loops (~3 MB). `Fx.explosion` forwards to `Sfx` so every blast sounds without per-caller hooks; drone and vehicle loops are attached by polling, not by the sim scripts. The `AudioListener3D` sits at the camera rig's ground focus, not on the camera boom.
- **Performance:** spotting is staggered across frames. A full 10-minute battle simulates headless in about 2 s.

**Test hooks:**
- `godot --headless --path . --fixed-fps 30 --quit-after 6000 -- autostart fast log` plays a whole battle with the player passive and prints the event log and result; with `kind=`/`seed=`/`sim=` fixed the run is reproducible bit for bit. An occasional `Leaked instance: AudioStreamPlayback…` warning at exit is engine shutdown noise (audio still mixing at quit), not a real leak.
- `-- shots=/dir` (non-headless) saves screenshots at fixed frames; `look=x,z,zoom` frames a ground point, `hit=x,z,power,radius` lands a structural blast before the second shot, `season=winter` overrides the mission's season.
